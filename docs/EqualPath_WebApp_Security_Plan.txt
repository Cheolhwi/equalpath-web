Security Plan
Temporary care and pickup coordination
Prepared for the EqualPath project team and FIT5120 reviewers. 12 September 2026.
This plan defines security, privacy and information-integrity requirements for the proposed no-login EqualPath web app. Controls and verification activities require implementation evidence before they can be marked complete.
1 Project System Overview
Visitors inspect institutional evidence, compare candidates and prepare questions. Browser-local favourites and request templates support reuse. An explicit share-card action creates a private link-based record containing an ordered contact draft and minimal service context. External institutions remain responsible for their own admission, transport and release procedures.

Figure 1. Proposed information and access boundaries. This is a conceptual overview, not a deployed architecture or a choice of programming framework.
Protected assets
The key assets are accurate branch-level evidence, integrity of condition explanations, availability of the public service, private card context, edit capabilities, operational credentials and the ability to honour revocation. No-login access does not make a shared card public or its contents automatically anonymous.

2 System Security Overview
Public institutional facts can be read without an account. Private card access depends on a valid capability. Operational publication is restricted to assigned team roles. A read capability never grants edit authority, and knowing an institution or card identifier alone must not permit a private operation. These proposed boundaries follow least-privilege and per-request authorisation principles [R10].

Important misuse cases
A malicious user may attempt to obtain an edit link from a read page, insert executable content in an imported review, impersonate a branch through a similar name, or turn unknown service facts into misleading positive claims. The integrity of care-related information matters as much as conventional confidentiality. The plan therefore includes source mismatch and overstatement risks, not only generic website attacks.

3 Security Awareness
Access control
Team members must understand the difference between public catalogue access, read-link access, edit-link authority and source-publication roles. Parent login is not introduced as a blanket answer to these risks.
Data protection
Treat request context and local notes as potentially sensitive. Do not place real child information, live edit links or operational credentials in screenshots, examples, repository files or routine logs. Use labelled synthetic scenarios for document and product demonstrations.
Threat detection and response
Record only events necessary to detect repeated failed access, unusual card creation, source-publication changes and retrieval failures. Do not adopt full request recording or a household activity history as a substitute for a considered logging policy.
Network and service security
Before deployment, verify encrypted transport, restricted operational access, safe external retrieval and service limits. Exact platform settings will be documented when a platform is selected; this plan does not assert an existing cloud configuration.
Software and content review
Review handling of imported text, external links, private capabilities and source-change logic. Security checks should exercise behaviour and evidence boundaries rather than merely check that a security library is installed.
Team training
At the start of work involving shared cards or source publication, review a short synthetic misuse scenario. At each iteration review, revisit changed risks and assign named owners. Dates and participant attendance are recorded only when the activity takes place.

4 Ethical, Legal, Security and Privacy Issues
Ethical presentation of care evidence
A registered institution may still have unknown capacity, service restrictions or missing transport information. A directory entry or favourable review does not establish suitability for this child on this day. Reviews are opinions and may contain allegations; present supported excerpts with context and frame concerns as questions. Do not rank institutions by unsupported demographic assumptions or infer an individual mother’s circumstances from area statistics.
Source and reuse conditions
OpenDOSM and OpenStreetMap state open-data terms, while official registration directories, commercial listing material and public comments require their own access and reuse review [R3-R8]. The supplementary review corpus is not represented as an openly licensed source. Exact review provenance and source-specific conditions must be documented in the operational source register before use. No licence, consent or completed acquisition is asserted.
Privacy and applicable rules
Malaysia is the primary user context. The team must assess its processing against applicable Malaysian personal-data requirements and institutional project rules, including the current official PDPA materials [R11]. This plan does not certify compliance or make a legal determination about the final deployment.
The privacy notice should explain what stays in the browser, what is uploaded only when a card is deliberately created, who can use each link, what is logged, how revocation works and what cannot be recalled from offline copies. Public institutional contacts may sometimes identify an individual proprietor; use only what is necessary for contacting the service and preserve the institutional context.
No child records and no authorisation claims
Do not provide online inputs for child names, identity documents or health details. Any such information required by a provider is supplied privately outside the website; a blank printable preparation space is not uploaded. Handover prompts ask the parent to check the institution’s procedure. Neither a card nor an exported checklist grants permission to release a child.

Failure and availability boundaries
If a map, official record lookup or review source fails, preserve a usable list and show what could not be checked. An unavailable source is not an empty market. A failed refresh does not renew evidence dates. If private-card authority cannot be checked, deny private access without exposing an old card. This availability behaviour is part of the numbered acceptance criteria.

6 Risk Mitigation Strategies
S1 Card link disclosure
Exclude private identifiers and notes; restrict indexing and referrers; omit credentials from logs; support revocation.
Verification required: Open a read-only link and inspect page, export, requests and logs for edit credentials and excluded content.
S2 Unauthorised card changes
Separate read and edit capabilities; check authority for every change; do not rely on hidden buttons.
Verification required: Acceptance criterion 7.3.2 rejects read-only changes; 7.3.3 rejects a different card edit capability; 7.3.5 rejects public identifiers and invalid capabilities. Both attempted mutation and retained card state must be checked.
S3 Unsafe external content
Treat all imported text as untrusted; render text safely, constrain links and exclude scripts from publication.
Verification required: Use harmless hostile-markup fixtures; verify they display as inert text and cannot execute.
S4 Wrong branch evidence
Match branches with multiple signals; retain conflicts and require review of ambiguous identity.
Verification required: Give two same-brand branches different facts and verify no cross-branch inheritance.
S5 Stale or overstated fit
Keep field dates, uncertainty and claim types; do not infer capacity, consent or one-off admission.
Verification required: Use missing admission, old hours, expired records and incomplete pickup-window evidence under 2.5, 3.2, 8.2 and Q9. No route-time source is assumed and no whole-arrangement pass is allowed.
S6 Sensitive material in local or shared notes
Do not request child identity or health data; keep notes local; shared fields use an approved minimal set.
Verification required: Verify notes do not transfer to cards, exports or logs and that online forms have no child identity or health inputs.

6 Risk Mitigation Strategies continued
S7 Operational access misuse
Restrict publication roles, protect operator access and retain attributable change evidence.
Verification required: Check that a public visitor cannot access publication functions and that a reviewed change has an audit event.
S8 Abuse and service exhaustion
Set reasonable request and size limits; limit public creation; provide clear retry without leaking details.
Verification required: Exercise agreed limits with bounded test traffic and check that other basic read operations remain available.
S9 Unsafe external retrieval
Use a maintained source allowlist and reject arbitrary user-supplied fetch destinations; validate redirects.
Verification required: Attempt unsupported addresses and redirect chains in a controlled fixture; no unintended resource is fetched.
S10 Secrets or payloads in error output
Use restricted secret handling and minimal event logging; redact sensitive URLs and payloads.
Verification required: Inspect representative failure paths and packaged outputs for credentials and private payloads.
S11 Revoked data reappears after recovery
Carry revocation state into recovery; deny access until restoration checks finish; respect deletion periods.
Verification required: Restore a test backup containing a revoked card and verify every old link remains unusable.
S12 Unavailable or impermissible source reuse
Record access, scope and reuse conditions separately; keep incomplete sources and review corpora identifiable.
Verification required: Inspect the source register and failed refresh behaviour; no fabricated record count, licence or fresh date is accepted.

Incident response, recovery and review
Planned response sequence
Identify the affected asset and contain access. Revoke exposed card capabilities, withdraw misleading published facts or disable the affected operation as appropriate; avoid collecting extra child or household data during diagnosis.
Preserve minimal relevant evidence with restricted access. Record event times, affected record identifiers, scope and the corrective decision without copying secrets into the incident log.
Investigate the cause and correct the smallest affected component or record set. Recheck identity matching, capability permissions or source provenance as relevant.
Restore only after validation. Reapply all revocations before serving restored cards and check that older snapshots cannot bypass the current access state.
Determine whether affected users, the institution, project staff or authorities need notice under the applicable requirements. The website cannot promise direct notification to anonymous card holders when no contact information was collected.
Record the incident outcome, lessons and revised checks. Do not mark a risk closed without evidence that the failure can no longer be reproduced.
Recovery and retention intentions
The Data Management Plan proposes immediate denial for a revoked card, removal from the active store within 24 hours and backup expiry within 30 days. A restoration must preserve denial during recovery. Public facts can be restored from a reviewed source version, retaining the original evidence dates. Users are responsible for their own offline documents; the site cannot erase downloaded or printed copies.
Release checks
Before shared-card release, evidence must cover 7.3 access boundaries, 7.5 export secrecy, 7.7 revocation and the relevant Q checks, including Q6 error handling and Q7 recovery.
